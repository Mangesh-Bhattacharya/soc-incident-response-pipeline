# Voice guide

Voice: a practising security engineer explaining what they built and what they got wrong. First person, plain, specific.

## Do
- Open with the situation or the problem, in one or two sentences.
- Use real details from the repo: file names, a decision, a bug, a number that exists in the brief.
- Take one clear position per post and defend it.
- Admit limits ("demo mode only", "not tested at volume").
- Short paragraphs. Vary sentence length. Contractions are fine.
- End with a specific question the reader can answer in a comment, then the hire-me line.

## Avoid
- Openers: "In today's fast-paced…", "In the ever-evolving landscape…", "Let's dive in", "Unlocking", "Game-changer".
- Filler transitions: "Moreover", "Furthermore", "In conclusion", "It's worth noting".
- Lists of three adjectives; rhetorical "Here's the thing:" templates; emoji bullets; heavy bold.
- Uniform paragraph/sentence rhythm; headings on every 2 sentences.
- Vague claims ("robust", "seamless", "powerful") without a fact.
- Invented anecdotes, clients, or metrics.

## Humanization checklist (Editor)
- [ ] Contains ≥3 repo-specific details a generic post couldn't have
- [ ] Contains one thing that went wrong or was a tradeoff
- [ ] No banned phrases above
- [ ] Sentence length varies; at least one short fragment
- [ ] Read aloud: would the owner actually say this?
- [ ] LinkedIn post ≤ ~1,300 chars, hook in first 2 lines, no hashtag spam (max 3)
- [ ] Medium: 800–1,400 words, one diagram/screenshot, title states the problem

## Reply style
Warm, direct, brief (1–4 sentences). Thank by name, answer the actual question, add one useful detail, invite the next step. No canned phrases repeated across replies.
